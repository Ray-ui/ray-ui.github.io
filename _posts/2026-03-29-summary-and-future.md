Before any new hardware paradigm emerges, indoor localization is already a fairly mature and well-understood problem.  
But a simple question remains: why isn’t it everywhere yet?

The answer likely goes beyond algorithms. It has more to do with the surrounding ecosystem and how the technology fits into real-world use.

There is no widely accepted benchmark that makes performance, cost, and deployment trade-offs clear.

There is no strong data ecosystem built on continuous collection and maintenance, most work still relies on static datasets.

And hardware and signal interfaces remain fragmented across devices and vendors, making scalable deployment difficult.

In that sense, the bottleneck may no longer be the technology itself, but everything around it.
